Plain-English Fire Action Assessment for the Northwoods

This page explains how the Northwoods Fire Action Assessment (FAA) works in everyday language. It is a screening conversation tool for northern Michigan, Wisconsin, and Minnesota’s Arrowhead — not a prescription, not NEPA, and not a stand-level plan.

Hexes are large (~10,000 acres). Each hex gets one primary action. Among places that are neither “defer” nor “wetlands — assess locally,” a Goldilocks priority flags what to talk about first.

What question does FAA answer?

Where should partners start a conversation about what to do about wildfire risk — and what kind of conversation is it?

Examples of conversation types:

  • Protect a timber plantation (or insert your values here) from fire damage
  • Treat fuels and homes where people and exposure both run high
  • Focus on ecosystem / fire-adapted systems away from dense housing
  • Pause and assess wetlands locally
  • Defer / monitor for now

Inputs (what we look at)

Input Plain meaning
Wildfire exposure (WFE) Where fire can transmit embers across the landscape today
People (WRTC Housing Unit Risk) Where homes and community wildfire risk are high
Plantations Managed tree farms treated as timber assets (from LANDFIRE EVT)
Peat / wetlands Peat and similar wetlands that need a local call (from EVT)
Pine / oak list Short EVT list; any of top 3 EVTs + people Moderate/Low/VL → ecosystem safety net
EVT composition (top 3) Largest three vegetation types by area in the hex — the fuel mosaic behind WFE
Recent disturbance (FDist) Fuel-add / remove — map layer only (brown = add, green = remove); not in score
PAD (protected lands) Share of the hex in GAP 1–3 conserved land — map context only
BpS / MFRI Historic fire-return context — popup only, does not pick the action

How WFE is built (short)

Each LANDFIRE EVT class has an ember-transmission score (about 0–100). Those scores are mapped to the landscape, then a neighborhood (focal) blend softens isolated hot pixels and reinforces continuous hot fuels — in the spirit of Beverly and others. Hex MEAN is the average of that blended surface; WFE_CAT is the published Low → Very High label from the same product.

So WFE is vegetation-based exposure, not a separate climate or ignition model. A hardwoods-majority hex can still score relatively high if enough high-transmission types (or neighbors) sit in the mosaic after blending. That is why we show top 3 EVTs on the action map popup after you re-run script 03.

What “high” means

Cutoffs are relative to this Northwoods AOI (recomputed each scoring run).

People bins (PEOPLE_CAT)

Same five labels as WFE: Very Low, Low, Moderate, High, Very High — from AOI quintiles of Housing Unit Risk (20/40/60/80th percentiles). In a low-density region, roughly one-fifth of hexes will always land in each bin — including High / Very High — even when absolute home density is modest. The bins rank where people are relatively more present within this AOI, not where cities are.
Treat for people still needs High or Very High people and High/VH WFE.

Wildfire exposure

Use WFE_CAT High or Very High only. No MEAN percentile bypass for actions.

Fuel change (score + map)

FDIST_FUEL_DELTA multiplies the Goldilocks score (add raises more than remove lowers) and is also shown as its own layer (brown = net add, green = net remove).

How each hex gets one action

Rules are checked in order. The first match wins.

Order Action When
1 Value to protect from fire Mostly a plantation
2 Wetlands — assess locally Mostly peat / listed wetland
3 Treat fire risk for people High/VH WFE and High/VH people
4 Ecosystem health focus High/VH WFE (people not High/VH)
5 Ecosystem health focus Pine/oak in top 3 and people Moderate / Low / Very Low
6 Defer and Monitor Nothing above matched

PAD and BpS never pick the action. Fuel never picks the action.

Worked examples

1. Plantation → Value to protect from fire.

2. Relativity High Home Density for the AOI (High/VH people) + High WFE → Treat fire risk for people.

3. Remote pine, Moderate or lower people → Ecosystem health focus.

4. Quiet WFE, High/VH people → Defer. Homes alone do not treat.

5. Quiet WFE, pine in top 3, Moderate or lower people → Ecosystem via pine safety net.

6. Quiet WFE, pine in top 3, High/VH people → Defer (tighten).

7. Typical quiet hardwoods → Defer.

Goldilocks (what to talk about first)

We cannot treat everything. Each hex gets an urgency score (homes weigh most, then exposure and plantations) × an asymmetric fuel-change multiplier, across the full AOI. Hexes with High/VH Wildfire Exposure Risk or high fuel-add get a score floor (about the 40th percentile of AOI scores) so remote hot places are not near-zero. PAD does not boost that score.

The Goldilocks map ranks actionable hexes only (not defer, not wetlands-assess-locally) with a white → dark purple heat from percentile rank of urgency (softened so extremes don’t wash out the map). Defer / wetlands draw grey and are not in that ranking — so high-housing defer hexes (e.g. Moderate WFE + many homes) do not look like top priorities. Use Basemap only to hide hex fills.

Signal Meaning
Heat (fill) Percentile rank among actionable hexes (0–100)
Grey Defer or wetlands — outside Goldilocks pool
Basemap only No hex fills — explore the basemap

Action = what kind of conversation. Goldilocks = relative urgency among places with an actionable conversation.

What FAA is not

  • Not ownership permission or tribal consultation
  • Not a burn plan or silvicultural prescription
  • Not “paint every high-housing hex as treat” without High/VH exposure
  • Not a claim that majority EVT must match the action — WFE is a mosaic measure
